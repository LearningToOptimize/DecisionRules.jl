Long-term hydrothermal planning

This case study applies TS-DDR to the long-term hydrothermal dispatch of the Bolivian interconnected system, with every stage subject to the AC power-flow equations, and compares it with stochastic dual dynamic programming, with two-stage linear decision rules, and with four model-free reinforcement-learning algorithms. All policies are trained on the same model and evaluated on a common set of paired inflow scenarios. The case, the JuMP and ExaModels engines, the training schedules, the baselines and the evaluation protocol are distributed with the examples of this package and of DecisionRulesExa.jl.

Motivation

Long-term hydrothermal scheduling determines, at weekly stages over a horizon of several years, the releases of the hydro plants and the dispatch of the thermal units that minimize the expected operating cost under uncertain inflows. Hydro generation has no direct cost; its economic value is the opportunity cost of the stored water, the expected thermal cost it displaces in later stages. The problem is the one for which SDDP was developed, and SDDP remains the method used in the operational planning of large hydro-dominated systems. Its cuts are valid lower approximations of the cost-to-go only when the stage problems are convex. The AC power-flow equations are not, so in practice the cost-to-go is computed on a convex relaxation or approximation of the network while decisions are taken on the physical one. The resulting mismatch between planning and operation appears as unexpected reservoir depletion and price spikes, and the convex model that yields the best policy is not known before each candidate has been trained and simulated.

The Bolivian system is chosen so that this mismatch is small. Its transfers follow essentially radial corridors, and on radial networks the second-order-cone relaxation of the power-flow equations is known to be accurate. SDDP with conic cuts is therefore a strong benchmark on this system, and the case study measures how closely a policy trained against the AC equations, with no convex model in its training, approaches it. The linear decision rule is trained with the same gradient, schedule and feasibility map as TS-DDR and isolates the role of the functional form of the policy; the reinforcement-learning agents act on the same stage problems and isolate the role of the dual gradient as the learning signal.

Organization

The problem states the reservoir dynamics, the AC network model, the planning problem and the stage problem solved by a target-state policy, and describes the Bolivian instance. Valuing water: two approaches presents SDDP with inconsistent formulations and TS-DDR, the two further baselines, the training schedule and the selection protocol, and lists what is held identical across methods. Reproducing the study describes how the policies are trained and evaluated with the distributed code, and the Walkthrough builds the stage problems, the policy and a few gradient steps on a short horizon on the CPU. The entry point to the code is the examples README.
